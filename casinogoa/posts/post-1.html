Poker in Goa: a beginner's guide to the tables

Poker looks intimidating from the rail — the jargon, the staring, the mountain of chips. But almost every regular at our tables started exactly where you are: curious, slightly nervous, and holding a hand they didn't fully understand. Here's everything you need for your first night.

Know your hands (in 60 seconds)

From strongest to weakest: royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card. Tape the order to your memory — or just ask the dealer. Beginners ask about hand strength constantly, and nobody minds.

Blinds, betting and the flow of a hand

Two players post forced bets called blinds before cards are dealt. Then everyone gets two cards, and betting moves clockwise through four rounds — pre-flop, flop (three community cards), turn (fourth card) and river (fifth card). You can check, bet, call, raise or fold. That's the whole game: make the best five-card hand, or convince everyone else you have.

“The best beginners aren't the luckiest — they're the most patient.”

Table manners that matter

Act in turn, keep your cards visible, don't discuss your hand while others are playing, and never splash the pot — place your chips neatly in front of you. Tip the dealer when you win a decent pot; it's the custom everywhere poker is played.

Your first session, simplified

Start at the lowest-limit cash table, buy in for the table minimum, and play only strong starting hands. Fold often — folding is free, and it's the most profitable move beginners underuse. Set a time limit and a loss limit before you sit down, and keep both.
