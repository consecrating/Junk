Andar Bahar: India’s favourite card game, explained

Long before the big casino floors, Andar Bahar was the card game of Indian households — played at festivals, weddings and long evenings with family. On the casino floor it keeps that same spirit: simple, social and over in a flash.

How a round plays

The dealer places one card face-up in the middle — the joker. Then cards are dealt alternately to two boxes marked Andar (inside) and Bahar (outside). The side that first receives a card matching the joker’s rank wins. That is the whole game.

Where the betting happens

You simply pick a side: Andar or Bahar. Payouts adjust depending on how many cards were dealt before the match — an early match on your side pays more. Some tables offer side bets on the number of cards dealt, for players who like a little extra spice.

“Fifty-fifty, pure and simple — Andar Bahar is chance at its most elegant.”

Why players love it

Rounds take under a minute, the rules need no memorising, and the whole table rides the same suspense together. If you grew up watching it at home, playing it properly — with a dealer, a crowd and the Mandovi outside — is a small homecoming.

Andar Bahar runs nightly on the main floor. Reserve your evening and take your side.
