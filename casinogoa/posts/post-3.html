Casino etiquette: the unwritten rules of the floor

Every casino floor runs on a set of rules nobody announces — and everyone notices when you break them. The good news: they're all common sense once someone explains them. Consider this your briefing.

Buying in and cashing out

Join a table between hands, place your cash on the felt (never hand it directly to the dealer) and state your buy-in. When you leave, wait for a pause in play, then ask the dealer to "color up" — they'll exchange your small chips for larger ones that are easier to carry to the cashier.

Hands, phones and chips

Don't touch your bet once the game is in motion. Keep phones off the table — most casinos prohibit them at live games. Handle your own cards with one hand in games where touching is allowed, and never touch another player's chips. When in doubt, ask the dealer; they'd rather explain than correct.

“Good etiquette isn't about formality — it's about respecting everyone's game.”

Tipping and temper

Tipping the dealer after a nice win is the universal custom — a small chip slid their way says everything. And whatever the cards do, keep your cool. Berating the dealer, celebrating opponents' losses or slowing the game down are the fastest ways to wear out a welcome.

The golden rule

Watch a table for a few minutes before sitting down. You'll absorb the pace, the minimums and the mood without a word — and you'll sit down looking like you belong. Because you do.
